About Me

I am Shaurya Baruah, a Computer Science & Electrical Engineering student at the University of Michigan College of Engineering.

I've built multiple real-world projects, including startups, nonprofit initiatives, and production-level software, and I understand both:

  • How to build technically strong projects
  • How those projects translate into competitive college applications

Through my own college application experience and mentoring work, I've seen firsthand that depth, initiative, and real-world impact are what differentiate strong applicants at top schools.

My mentorship focuses on helping students build work that clearly demonstrates those qualities.

Who This Is For

  • High school students interested in Computer Science, Engineering, or Technology
  • Students who want to stand out beyond grades and test scores
  • Students who are motivated but don't know what project to build or how to execute it well
  • Families aiming for top-tier colleges where depth and initiative matter

What Students Gain

  • Personalized, high-quality project ideas aligned with their interests
  • Guidance to build real, technically difficult projects (not tutorials)
  • Weekly accountability and structured progress
  • Experience solving hard, real-world problems
  • Clear framing of the work for college resumes, application essays, and interviews

The goal is not to "learn to code," but to build something real that demonstrates initiative, depth, and ownership.

How the Mentorship Works

Weekly 60-minute 1-on-1 Zoom sessions

Projects are:

  • Student-owned
  • Not part of any program or competition
  • Fully creditable to the student

Sessions focus on:

  • Project design and scoping
  • Technical decision-making
  • Execution and iteration
  • Translating work into application-ready narratives

Typical Roadmap

A structured 4-month journey from idea to impact

Month 1 – Direction & Foundation

  • Identify interests and long-term goals
  • Select a strong project idea
  • Scope appropriately (not too small, not unrealistic)
  • Begin core build

Month 2 – Deep Build

  • Focused development of core functionality
  • Technical depth and problem-solving
  • Iteration and refinement

Month 3 – Polish & Impact

  • Improve reliability, usability, and performance
  • Introduce real users or real-world use cases
  • Measure and articulate impact

Month 4 – Scale or Second Project

  • Expand scope and impact or
  • Begin a second, complementary project

What "Impact" Means

Impact does not mean a school club or a simple demo.

Impact means:

  • Real users
  • Real problems
  • Measurable outcomes (e.g., users, usage, adoption, results)

"I built something that people actually use."

This allows students to clearly articulate their impact in applications.
